## 1. Purpose and Scope

This glossary defines the foundational scientific, mathematical, and algorithmic terminology utilized throughout OVIC EON Phase 1 research documentation (Sub-phases 1.1 through 1.11). Consistent nomenclature is mandatory across all research deliverables, engineering data contracts, and architectural specifications to eliminate conceptual ambiguity between physical sensor observations, statistical pattern classifications, cognitive inferences, memory lifecycles, and robotic control behaviors.

## 2. Foundations of Affective Computing and Emotion Science

### Affective Computing
An interdisciplinary field spanning computer science, cognitive psychology, and neuroscience focused on systems capable of observing, interpreting, synthesizing, and adapting to human emotional phenomena and communicative displays (Picard, 1997). Within OVIC EON, affective computing is restricted to computational analysis of observable verbal, acoustic, and behavioral expressions, without asserting privileged or infallible insight into subjective inner states.

### Emotion Recognition
The computational process of capturing multi-sensory human behavioral signals (such as facial movements, vocal prosody, bodily gestures, or linguistic choices) and mapping them via statistical or neural classifiers to standardized affective categories or continuous psychological coordinates.

### Recognition vs. Classification vs. Detection vs. Modeling vs. Prediction vs. Understanding
To maintain strict scientific and safety integrity, EON distinguishes these related processes:
- **Detection:** Determining the spatial or temporal presence of a physical signal or landmark (e.g., detecting a human face bounding box, voice activity, or a hand coordinate).
- **Classification:** Assigning an observed static or windowed feature representation to one of $N$ predefined discrete categories (e.g., classifying an acoustic segment as "anger" with softmax probability).
- **Recognition:** The end-to-end identification and temporal association of an expression pattern over time, matching incoming patterns against known perceptual prototypes.
- **Modeling:** Constructing and maintaining a mathematical representation of dynamic internal or social state (e.g., tracking an interaction partner's valence trajectory over a 5-minute dialogue).
- **Prediction:** Forecasting future affective trajectories or interaction turns based on historical state context (e.g., predicting that an escalating vocal pitch signals imminent conversational breakdown).
- **Understanding:** The highest-level synthesis combining sensory observations, contextual world knowledge, conversational history, and cultural norms into actionable semantic context. In EON, "understanding" is an emergent property of the multi-engine pipeline, whereas perception engines strictly produce bounded observations.

### Categorical Emotion Model
A psychological framework postulating that human emotion is organized into discrete, universal, biologically hard-wired categories (Ekman, 1992). The canonical baseline consists of six basic emotions: *Joy/Happiness*, *Sadness*, *Anger*, *Fear*, *Disgust*, and *Surprise*, supplemented by *Neutral*. While highly interpretable for user interfaces, categorical models struggle with complex, mixed, or subtle workplace affects.

### Dimensional Emotion Model
A psychological framework representing affective states as continuous coordinates in a low-dimensional Euclidean space. The predominant formulation is Russell's Circumplex Model (Russell, 1980):
- **Valence ($V \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The degree of positive or negative affect, ranging from extreme unpleasantness/distress ($-1.0$) to extreme pleasure/joy ($+1.0$).
- **Arousal ($A \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The physiological and neurobiological activation level, ranging from deep sleep/calm ($-1.0$) to intense excitement/panic ($+1.0$).
- **Dominance ($D \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The perceived sense of control, agency, or submissiveness, ranging from feeling overwhelmed/submissive ($-1.0$) to feeling authoritative/empowered ($+1.0$). Also termed the VAD space (Mehrabian, 1996).

### Appraisal Theory
Cognitive theories of emotion—most notably Scherer's Component Process Model (CPM; Scherer, 2005)—positing that emotions are not direct reactions to raw sensory events, but are generated by continuous, hierarchical evaluations ("appraisals") of an event's novelty, pleasantness, goal relevance, coping potential, and norm compatibility.

## 3. Computer Vision and Facial Expression Analysis

### Facial Action Coding System (FACS)
An anatomically grounded, objective taxonomy developed by Ekman and Friesen (1978) that deconstructs every visually distinguishable human facial movement into discrete units termed **Action Units (AUs)** based on the underlying contraction or relaxation of specific facial muscles.

### Action Unit (AU)
A specific, standardized muscular movement index defined in FACS. Examples:
- **AU1:** Inner Brow Raiser (*Frontalis, pars medialis*)
- **AU2:** Outer Brow Raiser (*Frontalis, pars lateralis*)
- **AU4:** Brow Lowerer (*Corrugator supercilii, Depressor supercilii*)
- **AU6:** Cheek Raiser (*Orbicularis oculi, pars orbitalis*)
- **AU12:** Lip Corner Puller (*Zygomaticus major*)
- **AU15:** Lip Corner Depressor (*Depressor anguli oris*)
- **AU25:** Lips Part (*Depressor labii inferioris, or relaxation of Orbicularis oris*)

### AU Intensity
The quantitative grading of muscle activation in FACS, traditionally scored on a five-point ordinal scale from $A$ (trace) to $E$ (maximum). In computational systems, AU intensity is modeled as continuous real values $\in [0.0, 1.0]$ or $[0.0, 5.0]$.

### Facial Landmarks
Distinct geometric coordinate points located on key anatomical facial structures (e.g., eye corners, nasal bridge, eyebrow contour, lip boundary, jawline). Standard configurations include:
- **68-point 2D Landmarks:** The classical iBUG / Dlib mark configuration.
- **468-point / 478-point 3D Face Mesh:** Dense 3D surface landmarks (as in MediaPipe Face Mesh) capturing high-fidelity surface topology and iris tracking.

### Ephemeral Tracking ID
A transient, locally unique spatial-temporal identifier (e.g., generated via ByteTrack or SORT algorithms) assigned to a detected human within an active video stream. EON strictly mandates that tracking IDs expire when the subject leaves sensor field-of-view, prohibiting cross-session biometric template storage or facial re-identification.

## 4. Acoustic Features and Speech Processing

### Speech Emotion Recognition (SER)
The automated classification or dimensional regression of a speaker's affective state directly from audio signals, isolating vocal acoustic patterns independent of textual transcriptions.

### Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
The transcription of spoken phonetic audio into written linguistic text (e.g., Whisper, Vosk), distinct from SER. While ASR extracts *what* was spoken (lexical content), SER extracts *how* it was spoken (paralinguistic acoustic expression).

### Voice Activity Detection (VAD)
An algorithmic pipeline (e.g., Silero VAD, WebRTC VAD) that partitions continuous audio streams into segments of active human speech versus non-speech (silence, robot actuator hum, environmental background noise).

### Prosody
The suprasegmental acoustic properties of spoken language that span across multiple phonetic syllables or words, encompassing variations in pitch, rhythm, tempo, loudness, and stress.

### Fundamental Frequency ($F_0$)
The lowest frequency of periodic vocal fold vibration during voiced speech, perceived psychologically as vocal pitch. Key SER metrics include mean $F_0$, $F_0$ dynamic range, jitter, and pitch contour slope.

### Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)
A compact spectral feature representation derived from the short-time Fourier transform of audio. Power spectra are mapped onto the non-linear perceptual Mel scale and transformed via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to capture the vocal tract transfer function while decoupling excitation harmonics.

### Extended Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set (eGeMAPS)
A standardized, scientifically curated acoustic feature set comprising 88 acoustic and prosodic statistics (Eyben et al., 2016), designed specifically for paralinguistic voice analysis and speech emotion classification.

### Ephemeral Raw Audio Buffer
A volatile memory region in EON where microphone PCM audio frames are temporarily held for real-time feature extraction and VAD analysis. Upon feature computation or window termination, raw audio frames are immediately flushed and overwritten, preventing disk persistence.

## 5. Multimodal Machine Learning and Fusion

### Multimodal Fusion
The mathematical and algorithmic integration of heterogeneous sensory streams (e.g., visual facial expressions, vocal acoustics, body posture, conversational text) into a unified perceptual representation.

### Early Fusion (Feature-Level)
The concatenation or direct joint projection of raw or pre-processed feature vectors from distinct modalities at the input stage before passing into a unified classification or regression network. Early fusion models cross-modal correlations early, but is vulnerable to missing modalities and temporal sampling mismatches.

### Intermediate Fusion (Representation-Level / Cross-Modal Attention)
The transformation of each modality through independent deep representation layers, followed by cross-attention or tensor fusion mechanisms that allow features from one modality to attend to and modulate representations in another modality prior to classification.

### Late Fusion (Decision-Level)
The independent inference of affective states by dedicated unimodal models, followed by a decision aggregation stage (such as weighted averaging, Bayesian evidence pooling, or ensemble voting) to produce the final system estimate. Late fusion is robust to modality dropout, but cannot discover complex non-linear cross-modal interactions.

### Hybrid Fusion (Hierarchical Reliability-Aware)
A multi-tier architecture combining unimodal feature extraction, intermediate cross-modal attention gating, and decision-level late fallback. In EON, Hybrid Fusion weights inputs according to real-time sensor quality indicators and modality-specific confidence values.

### Modality Dropout
A regularization and robustness technique applied during model training where specific sensor inputs (e.g., video or audio) are randomly zeroed out or masked. This forces the fusion architecture to maintain accurate predictions when one or more sensors fail or become occluded in real-world environments.

### Conflicting Affective Cues
A condition where distinct communicative modalities exhibit contradictory valence or arousal values (e.g., a smiling face paired with a hostile, strained vocal tone, indicating polite sarcasm or passive resistance). EON treats cross-modal divergence as an explicit diagnostic signal rather than averaging it into an erroneous "neutral" classification.

## 6. Human Activity Recognition and Spatial Perception

### Human Activity Recognition (HAR)
The automated detection and classification of human physical motions, postures, gestures, and interaction sequences using optical cameras, depth sensors, or wearable inertial sensors.

### Spatial Skeleton / Pose Estimation
The computer vision process of extracting 2D or 3D anatomical joint coordinates (e.g., nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) from video frames. Canonical frameworks include MediaPipe Pose (33 3D landmarks) and COCO Pose (17 2D landmarks).

### Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)
A deep learning architecture designed for skeleton-based action recognition. Human joint coordinates form the spatial graph vertices (connected by anatomical bones), and temporal edges link corresponding joints across successive frames, enabling joint convolution over space and time.

### Interaction Taxonomy
The hierarchical categorization of human activities into:
1. **Basic Activities:** Unimodal individual biomechanical actions (e.g., standing, walking, sitting, bending).
2. **Interaction Activities:** Communicative bodily signals directed at a robot or human partner (e.g., waving, pointing, nodding, beckoning).
3. **Human-Object Activities:** Interactions involving physical tools or environmental objects (e.g., typing, carrying a box, reading a book).
4. **Social Activities:** Multi-person interpersonal activities (e.g., handshake, conversation, queuing).

### Non-Inferential Interpretation
The architectural principle governing EON's perception layers, establishing that computer vision models output strictly descriptive physical motion labels (e.g., "rapid forward stride toward robot"), without inferring unobserved psychological intentions or safety clearance.

## 7. Uncertainty Quantification and Temporal Dynamics

### Aleatoric Uncertainty
Statistical uncertainty arising from inherent stochasticity, noise, or physical degradation in sensor observations (e.g., motion blur, lens flare, acoustic reverberation, microphone clipping, low illumination). Aleatoric uncertainty cannot be reduced by collecting more training data, but must be quantified per observation.

### Epistemic Uncertainty
Model uncertainty arising from lack of knowledge, novel scenarios, out-of-distribution human expressions, or unseen demographics. Epistemic uncertainty reflects the model's unfamiliarity with the input and can theoretically be reduced with additional representative training data.

### Confidence Calibration
The degree to which a machine learning model's predicted probability $P(\hat{Y} = y | X) = p$ matches the true empirical likelihood of correctness. Uncalibrated modern neural networks frequently produce overconfident softmax probabilities ($p \approx 0.99$) even when making incorrect predictions.

### Temperature Scaling
A post-processing calibration technique for classification models where logit vectors $z$ are divided by a single learned scalar parameter $T > 0$ before applying softmax: $\hat{p}_i = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_j e^{z_j / T}}$, correcting systemic overconfidence without altering model rank ordering.

### Dirichlet Calibration
An advanced multi-class probability calibration method that fits a Dirichlet distribution to the logit outputs, modeling class-dependent biases and correlation structures more flexibly than temperature scaling.

### Temporal Smoothing / Windowing
Techniques used to filter high-frequency sensor jitter and momentary misclassifications across consecutive frames. Methods include Exponential Moving Average (EMA), sliding window majority voting, Hidden Markov Models (HMM), and causal temporal convolutions.

## 8. Robotics Safety and Architectural Boundaries

### Non-Authoritative Actuation (`is_authoritative_stop=False`)
A fundamental safety invariant of OVIC EON stating that outputs generated by emotion recognition, social reasoning, or activity classification modules are non-authoritative recommendations. Under no circumstances may an affective observation trigger or override safety-critical emergency stops, collision avoidance maneuvers, or robot kinematic limits. Physical robot safety is strictly governed by independent, deterministic, hardware-certified safety controllers (ISO 13849 / ISO 10218 / ISO 15066).

### Deterministic Safety Controller
The real-time, certifiable execution environment on a robot responsible for trajectory generation, torque limits, obstacle collision avoidance, and E-Stop monitoring. EON operates strictly as an advisory intelligence overlay communicating via non-blocking asynchronous event envelopes.

## 9. Gesture and Posture Recognition (Sub-phase 1.6)

### Static Gesture
A communicative or symbolic spatial hand configuration held in a stable posture over a defined temporal window (e.g., open palm, closed fist, pointing finger, thumbs up, peace sign). Static gestures are characterized by relative inter-finger joint distances and palm orientations rather than limb motion trajectories.

### Dynamic Gesture
A communicative bodily or manual motion sequence defined by spatial trajectory, velocity profile, and rhythmic repetition across consecutive frames (e.g., waving hand, beckoning, reaching, waving away, stop signal). Dynamic gestures require temporal segmentation into preparation, stroke (nucleus), and retraction phases (Kendon, 2004).

### Pose Estimation
The computer vision process of localizing anatomical landmarks on a human body or hand in 2D image coordinates or 3D metric camera space (e.g., 33 body keypoints in MediaPipe Pose, 21 hand joints in MediaPipe Hands).

### Skeleton Tracking
The temporal association and spatial smoothing of skeletal joint graphs across video frames, maintaining persistent topological connectivity and computing instantaneous joint velocities $(\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z})$ and angular accelerations.

### Co-Speech Gesture
Non-verbal manual and bodily movements executed concurrently with spoken utterances to emphasize, illustrate, or complement semantic speech content (McNeill, 1992). Co-speech gestures exhibit tight temporal synchrony with acoustic pitch accents.

## 10. Computational Memory Systems (Sub-phase 1.7)

### Working Memory
A transient, low-capacity computational store maintaining the immediate operational state of an active interaction turn (e.g., current dialogue turn, recently observed visual objects, active robot task, unresolved user questions). Working memory is cleared or superseded upon session termination.

### Episodic Memory
An event-based, temporally indexed record of past interaction episodes, tasks completed, and significant conversational milestones associated with an authorized user session. Episodic memory stores *what* happened, *when*, and in what environmental context, strictly bounded by retention policies.

### Semantic Memory
A structured, non-temporal knowledge store encoding verified facts, confirmed user preferences (e.g., preferred name, language, interaction distance), environmental layout knowledge, and domain concepts explicitly approved for long-term retention.

### Procedural Memory
A deterministic store of validated interaction protocols, conversational workflows, task execution sequences, and safety guidelines that define *how* the robot performs collaborative tasks.

### Memory Consolidation
The computational pipeline that aggregates, summarizes, deduplicates, and validates transient working memory events into stable long-term episodic or semantic memory records. Memory consolidation must not elevate probabilistic sensory observations into permanent factual assertions without explicit verification.

### Memory Decay and Expiration
A privacy-preserving retention mechanism whereby episodic records are systematically assigned a Time-To-Live (TTL) or undergo exponential importance decay:
$$S(t) = S_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda t}$$
Expired records are purged across primary tables, inverted indices, vector stores, and volatile caches.

## 11. Empathy Reasoning and Perspective-Taking (Sub-phase 1.8)

### Cognitive Empathy
The computational capacity to recognize, model, and reason about another person's mental state, emotional situation, and perspective without requiring the reasoning system to simulate or experience visceral emotional affect.

### Affective Empathy
In biological entities, the involuntary vicarious sharing or mirroring of another's affective experience. EON **explicitly excludes** synthetic affective empathy claims, operating strictly through cognitive perspective-taking and rule-governed compassionate behavior.

### Compassionate Empathy (Empathic Concern)
A goal-directed behavioral disposition focused on understanding another person's situational difficulty and formulating supportive, non-intrusive actions to alleviate distress or assist task completion.

### Perspective-Taking (Theory of Mind)
The cognitive modeling process of representing another agent's perceptual viewpoint, knowledge boundaries, and possible beliefs (which may differ from the robot's own world knowledge or other participants' beliefs).

### Supportive Response Policy
A deterministic policy framework governing robot conversational behavior during human distress, prioritizing active listening, acknowledgment, respectful clarification, permission-seeking before intervention, and avoidance of patronizing or emotionally manipulative language.

## 12. Personality Modelling and Interaction Styles (Sub-phase 1.9)

### Personality Trait
A persistent, dimensional behavioral tendency describing an agent's typical style of social interaction across varying contexts. In psychological science, traits are predominantly operationalized via the Five-Factor Model (OCEAN: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism; Goldberg, 1990) or the HEXACO model.

### Interaction Style
A bounded, configurable set of communicative parameters (e.g., formality, verbosity, conversational initiative, humor level, empathy phrasing) defining the robot's behavioral expression. EON treats robot personality strictly as an **interaction style configuration**, never as an autonomous psychological entity.

### Persona Configuration
A structured, versioned specification defining a robot's role-specific social presentation (e.g., "Professional Medical Assistant", "Engaging STEM Tutor", "Calm Industrial Guide"), bound by manufacturer-defined behavioral guardrails.

### Unwanted Personality Drift
The undesirable divergence of a robot's conversational tone, style, or behavioral policies over extended interaction due to unconstrained reinforcement learning or user-prompt steering. EON prevents personality drift via immutable deterministic style constraints.

## 13. Social Interaction and Dialogue Systems (Sub-phase 1.10)

### Turn-Taking
The coordinated conversational protocol governing how interlocutors exchange speaking rights and listening roles. Turn-taking relies on subtle multimodal cues including vocal pauses, intonation drops, gaze shifts, and hand relaxations to prevent collision or prolonged silence (Sacks et al., 1974).

### Backchannel
Subtle, non-intrusive verbal or non-verbal signals (e.g., "mm-hmm", "I see", head nod, slight eyebrow raise) emitted by the listener during an ongoing speaker turn to signal continued attention, comprehension, and engagement without usurping the conversational floor.

### Proxemics
The study of spatial distances maintained by humans during interpersonal encounters, formalized by Edward T. Hall (1966) into Intimate ($0\text{--}0.45\text{m}$), Personal ($0.45\text{--}1.2\text{m}$), Social ($1.2\text{--}3.6\text{m}$), and Public ($>3.6\text{m}$) zones. In social robotics, proxemic behavior dictates safe, non-threatening approach trajectories.

### Dialogue Management
The central coordination component in a conversational system responsible for tracking dialogue state, managing task context, handling slot filling, resolving misunderstandings, and selecting conversational action intents.

### Social Signal Processing (SSP)
The automated analysis of non-verbal behavioral cues (gaze, head pose, proxemic distance, vocal pitch, posture) to interpret social dynamics, conversational interest, agreement, and interaction readiness (Vinciarelli et al., 2009).

## 14. Response Generation and Text-to-Speech (Sub-phase 1.11)

### Natural Language Generation (NLG)
The subfield of artificial intelligence focused on converting structured communicative intents, computational knowledge, or dialogue goals into coherent, grammatically correct, and contextually appropriate human language.

### Response Planning
The deterministic or rule-guided stage preceding surface text generation wherein the system determines *what* information must be communicated (content selection) and in what communicative posture (rhetorical structuring), constrained by safety policies.

### Retrieval-Based Generation
A generation methodology that constructs responses by querying a curated, human-reviewed library of approved response templates and utterance pairs, selecting candidates via semantic similarity or slot matching. Highly deterministic, safe, and controllable.

### Small Language Model (SLM)
A parameter-efficient transformer language model ($\sim 0.5\text{B}\text{--}3.5\text{B}$ parameters, such as Qwen2.5, Phi-3.5-mini, Gemma-2-2B, SmolLM) optimized for edge deployment. When quantized to INT4 or INT8, SLMs execute locally on modern multi-core CPUs without requiring cloud connectivity.

### Text-to-Speech (TTS)
The synthesis of acoustic speech waveforms from written text. Modern low-latency local TTS (such as Piper) leverages fast acoustic neural vocoders to synthesize expressive speech in real time on edge CPUs.

### Multimodal Response Coordination
The synchronized temporal scheduling of spoken audio playback, text display, screen avatar animation, robot head orientation, and physical gesture execution to produce a unified, coherent social response.

## 15. Open-Source Models and Weight Formats (Sub-phase 1.12)

### GGUF (GPT-Generated Unified Format)
A binary file format designed by Georgi Gerganov for storing and distributing quantized neural network weights (particularly for `llama.cpp`). GGUF stores both architecture hyperparameters and quantized tensor data in a single mmap-friendly file, enabling fast zero-copy loading on edge CPUs.

### Weight Quantization (Q4_K_M, INT8, FP16)
The mathematical reduction of numeric precision used to store neural network parameters. 4-bit (Q4_K_M) quantization reduces model memory footprints by $\sim 70\%$ relative to FP16 while maintaining $\ge 98\%$ of original conversational perplexity, enabling local execution of billion-parameter models within $\sim 1\text{ GB}$ of RAM.

### Small Language Model (SLM) Inference Engine
A specialized, lightweight inference engine (e.g., `llama.cpp`, ONNX Runtime GenAI) that compiles matrix multiplications using native CPU vector instructions (AVX2, AVX-512, ARM NEON) to achieve high token throughput on edge CPUs without requiring heavy Python or CUDA runtimes.

## 16. Commercial Licensing and Intellectual Property (Sub-phase 1.13)

### Weight-Code Licensing Decoupling
The legal and architectural principle recognizing that a permissive source-code license (such as MIT or Apache 2.0) applied to a software repository does not automatically grant commercial rights to pre-trained weight checkpoints hosted within or produced by that repository, especially if the weights were trained on proprietary, non-commercial, or unconsented corpora.

### Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)
A comprehensive, formally structured inventory of all software components, libraries, shared binaries, model weight files, and dependencies embedded within an application. EON utilizes standardized SPDX 2.3 and CycloneDX 1.5 specifications to automate legal compliance checks in CI/CD.

### Permissive Open-Core Licensing
A commercial software distribution model wherein the core infrastructure (event bus, schemas, base connectors) is released under permissive open-source licenses (Apache 2.0 / MIT), while commercial extensions (OEM-specific robot motion connectors, enterprise fleet managers) are packaged under commercial licenses.

## 17. Edge Hardware and Compute Architecture (Sub-phase 1.14)

### Memory Bandwidth Roofline
The theoretical upper limit on autoregressive language model token generation imposed by memory transfer speeds rather than raw arithmetic compute. Autoregressive inference loads every model weight parameter once per generated token, making dual-channel memory bus width a primary determinant of edge SLM throughput.

### SIMD Vectorization (AVX2, AVX-512, ARM NEON)
Single Instruction, Multiple Data hardware extensions in modern x86_64 and ARM64 processors that allow a single processor instruction to perform parallel arithmetic operations across vector registers (128-bit, 256-bit, or 512-bit wide), boosting CPU neural network matrix multiplication by $4\times\text{--}8\times$.

### Neural Processing Unit (NPU) Execution Provider
A vendor-specific acceleration runtime plugin (e.g., Intel OpenVINO NPU, Qualcomm QNN) that compiles and offloads neural network subgraphs directly to dedicated low-power matrix acceleration silicon on modern system-on-chip processors.

## 18. Edge Systems and Deployment Engineering (Sub-phase 1.15)

### POSIX Shared Memory Ring Buffer
A low-latency, zero-copy inter-process communication mechanism (`shm_open`, `mmap`) used to transfer uncompressed camera video frames from sensor ingestion drivers to vision perception processes without memory duplication or kernel context-switch penalties.

### Priority Ring Scheduling (SCHED_FIFO)
A deterministic real-time operating system process scheduling policy that guarantees critical tasks (audio capture, safety monitors) preempt lower-priority tasks (background language model decoding) to prevent acoustic stutter or delayed safety responses.

### Degraded Operating Mode
A pre-planned, deterministic fallback state engaged when edge hardware experiences thermal throttling, memory exhaustion, or sensor failure, ensuring the robot maintains graceful, safe, and communicative behavior without system lock-up.

## 19. ROS 2 Robotics Middleware Integration (Sub-phase 1.16)

### ROS 2 Managed Lifecycle Node
A ROS 2 node architecture governed by a formal state machine (`Unconfigured`, `Inactive`, `Active`, `Finalized`), ensuring that robotic perception and social intelligence nodes load heavy models and calibrate sensors before active communication begins.

### SROS2 Access Control Policy
A security framework implementing DDS-Security standards (OMG DDS-SEC) that cryptographically signs and restricts topic publishing rights, preventing cognitive or language nodes from publishing unauthorized direct motor velocity commands (`/cmd_vel`).

### Nav2 Social Costmap Layer
A 2D navigation costmap plugin that renders dynamic proxemic repulsion zones (Hall's personal and social interaction zones) around detected humans, enabling standard robot navigation planners to navigate comfortably around people without requiring custom path algorithms.

## 20. Comprehensive Technical Feasibility and Risk (Sub-phase 1.17)

### Zero Recurring API Liability
An economic and operational architectural principle wherein an autonomous system relies 100% on local edge intelligence models, completely eliminating recurring cloud AI API bills (\$0.00/month) and protecting commercial deployers from sudden price hikes or service shutdowns.

### Non-Authoritative Expressive Actuation
A fundamental robotics safety architectural rule stating that communicative gestures, facial expressions, and social movements generated by affective models are strictly advisory requests, subject to independent kinematic validation and immediate preemption by certified safety PLCs.

## 21. Model and Dataset Governance (Sub-phase 1.18)

### Cryptographic Checksum Pinning
The strict verification of model weight binaries against immutable SHA-256 cryptographic hashes during automated builds and deployment packaging, preventing accidental version drift, corrupted downloads, or supply-chain tampering.

### Cryptographic Erasure (Crypto-Shredding)
A privacy compliance mechanism for GDPR Article 17 ("Right to be Forgotten") wherein destroying a specific user's symmetric data encryption key (DEK) renders all historical episodic and relational records mathematically unrecoverable, even if raw storage sectors retain physical magnetic or flash data fragments.
