## 1. Purpose and Scope

This glossary defines the foundational scientific, mathematical, and algorithmic terminology utilized throughout OVIC EON Phase 1 research documentation (Sub-phases 1.1 through 1.5). Consistent nomenclature is mandatory across all research deliverables, engineering data contracts, and architectural specifications to eliminate conceptual ambiguity between physical sensor observations, statistical pattern classifications, cognitive inferences, and robotic control behaviors.

## 2. Foundations of Affective Computing and Emotion Science

### Affective Computing
An interdisciplinary field spanning computer science, cognitive psychology, and neuroscience focused on systems capable of observing, interpreting, synthesizing, and adapting to human emotional phenomena and communicative displays (Picard, 1997). Within OVIC EON, affective computing is restricted to computational analysis of observable verbal, acoustic, and behavioral expressions, without asserting privileged or infallible insight into subjective inner states.

### Emotion Recognition
The computational process of capturing multi-sensory human behavioral signals (such as facial movements, vocal prosody, bodily gestures, or linguistic choices) and mapping them via statistical or neural classifiers to standardized affective categories or continuous psychological coordinates.

### Recognition vs. Classification vs. Detection vs. Modeling vs. Prediction vs. Understanding
To maintain strict scientific and safety integrity, EON distinguishes these related processes:
- **Detection:** Determining the spatial or temporal presence of a physical signal or landmark (e.g., detecting a human face bounding box, voice activity, or a hand coordinate).
- **Classification:** Assigning an observed static or windowed feature representation to one of $N$ predefined discrete categories (e.g., classifying an acoustic segment as "anger" with softmax probability).
- **Recognition:** The end-to-end identification and temporal association of an expression pattern over time, matching incoming patterns against known perceptual prototypes.
- **Modeling:** Constructing and maintaining a mathematical representation of dynamic internal or social state (e.g., tracking an interaction partner's valence trajectory over a 5-minute dialogue).
- **Prediction:** Forecasting future affective trajectories or interaction turns based on historical state context (e.g., predicting that an escalating vocal pitch signals imminent conversational breakdown).
- **Understanding:** The highest-level synthesis combining sensory observations, contextual world knowledge, conversational history, and cultural norms into actionable semantic context. In EON, "understanding" is an emergent property of the multi-engine pipeline, whereas perception engines strictly produce bounded observations.

### Categorical Emotion Model
A psychological framework postulating that human emotion is organized into discrete, universal, biologically hard-wired categories (Ekman, 1992). The canonical baseline consists of six basic emotions: *Joy/Happiness*, *Sadness*, *Anger*, *Fear*, *Disgust*, and *Surprise*, supplemented by *Neutral*. While highly interpretable for user interfaces, categorical models struggle with complex, mixed, or subtle workplace affects.

### Dimensional Emotion Model
A psychological framework representing affective states as continuous coordinates in a low-dimensional Euclidean space. The predominant formulation is Russell's Circumplex Model (Russell, 1980):
- **Valence ($V \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The degree of positive or negative affect, ranging from extreme unpleasantness/distress ($-1.0$) to extreme pleasure/joy ($+1.0$).
- **Arousal ($A \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The physiological and neurobiological activation level, ranging from deep sleep/calm ($-1.0$) to intense excitement/panic ($+1.0$).
- **Dominance ($D \in [-1.0, +1.0]$):** The perceived sense of control, agency, or submissiveness, ranging from feeling overwhelmed/submissive ($-1.0$) to feeling authoritative/empowered ($+1.0$). Also termed the VAD space (Mehrabian, 1996).

### Appraisal Theory
Cognitive theories of emotion—most notably Scherer's Component Process Model (CPM; Scherer, 2005)—positing that emotions are not direct reactions to raw sensory events, but are generated by continuous, hierarchical evaluations ("appraisals") of an event's novelty, pleasantness, goal relevance, coping potential, and norm compatibility.

## 3. Computer Vision and Facial Expression Analysis

### Facial Action Coding System (FACS)
An anatomically grounded, objective taxonomy developed by Ekman and Friesen (1978) that deconstructs every visually distinguishable human facial movement into discrete units termed **Action Units (AUs)** based on the underlying contraction or relaxation of specific facial muscles.

### Action Unit (AU)
A specific, standardized muscular movement index defined in FACS. Examples:
- **AU1:** Inner Brow Raiser (*Frontalis, pars medialis*)
- **AU2:** Outer Brow Raiser (*Frontalis, pars lateralis*)
- **AU4:** Brow Lowerer (*Corrugator supercilii, Depressor supercilii*)
- **AU6:** Cheek Raiser (*Orbicularis oculi, pars orbitalis*)
- **AU12:** Lip Corner Puller (*Zygomaticus major*)
- **AU15:** Lip Corner Depressor (*Depressor anguli oris*)
- **AU25:** Lips Part (*Depressor labii inferioris, or relaxation of Orbicularis oris*)

### AU Intensity
The quantitative grading of muscle activation in FACS, traditionally scored on a five-point ordinal scale from $A$ (trace) to $E$ (maximum). In computational systems, AU intensity is modeled as continuous real values $\in [0.0, 1.0]$ or $[0.0, 5.0]$.

### Facial Landmarks
Distinct geometric coordinate points located on key anatomical facial structures (e.g., eye corners, nasal bridge, eyebrow contour, lip boundary, jawline). Standard configurations include:
- **68-point 2D Landmarks:** The classical iBUG / Dlib mark configuration.
- **468-point / 478-point 3D Face Mesh:** Dense 3D surface landmarks (as in MediaPipe Face Mesh) capturing high-fidelity surface topology and iris tracking.

### Ephemeral Tracking ID
A transient, locally unique spatial-temporal identifier (e.g., generated via ByteTrack or SORT algorithms) assigned to a detected human within an active video stream. EON strictly mandates that tracking IDs expire when the subject leaves sensor field-of-view, prohibiting cross-session biometric template storage or facial re-identification.

## 4. Acoustic Features and Speech Processing

### Speech Emotion Recognition (SER)
The automated classification or dimensional regression of a speaker's affective state directly from audio signals, isolating vocal acoustic patterns independent of textual transcriptions.

### Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
The transcription of spoken phonetic audio into written linguistic text (e.g., Whisper, Vosk), distinct from SER. While ASR extracts *what* was spoken (lexical content), SER extracts *how* it was spoken (paralinguistic acoustic expression).

### Voice Activity Detection (VAD)
An algorithmic pipeline (e.g., Silero VAD, WebRTC VAD) that partitions continuous audio streams into segments of active human speech versus non-speech (silence, robot actuator hum, environmental background noise).

### Prosody
The suprasegmental acoustic properties of spoken language that span across multiple phonetic syllables or words, encompassing variations in pitch, rhythm, tempo, loudness, and stress.

### Fundamental Frequency ($F_0$)
The lowest frequency of periodic vocal fold vibration during voiced speech, perceived psychologically as vocal pitch. Key SER metrics include mean $F_0$, $F_0$ dynamic range, jitter, and pitch contour slope.

### Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)
A compact spectral feature representation derived from the short-time Fourier transform of audio. Power spectra are mapped onto the non-linear perceptual Mel scale and transformed via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to capture the vocal tract transfer function while decoupling excitation harmonics.

### Extended Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set (eGeMAPS)
A standardized, scientifically curated acoustic feature set comprising 88 acoustic and prosodic statistics (Eyben et al., 2016), designed specifically for paralinguistic voice analysis and speech emotion classification.

### Ephemeral Raw Audio Buffer
A volatile memory region in EON where microphone PCM audio frames are temporarily held for real-time feature extraction and VAD analysis. Upon feature computation or window termination, raw audio frames are immediately flushed and overwritten, preventing disk persistence.

## 5. Multimodal Machine Learning and Fusion

### Multimodal Fusion
The mathematical and algorithmic integration of heterogeneous sensory streams (e.g., visual facial expressions, vocal acoustics, body posture, conversational text) into a unified perceptual representation.

### Early Fusion (Feature-Level)
The concatenation or direct joint projection of raw or pre-processed feature vectors from distinct modalities at the input stage before passing into a unified classification or regression network. Early fusion models cross-modal correlations early, but is vulnerable to missing modalities and temporal sampling mismatches.

### Intermediate Fusion (Representation-Level / Cross-Modal Attention)
The transformation of each modality through independent deep representation layers, followed by cross-attention or tensor fusion mechanisms that allow features from one modality to attend to and modulate representations in another modality prior to classification.

### Late Fusion (Decision-Level)
The independent inference of affective states by dedicated unimodal models, followed by a decision aggregation stage (such as weighted averaging, Bayesian evidence pooling, or ensemble voting) to produce the final system estimate. Late fusion is robust to modality dropout, but cannot discover complex non-linear cross-modal interactions.

### Hybrid Fusion (Hierarchical Reliability-Aware)
A multi-tier architecture combining unimodal feature extraction, intermediate cross-modal attention gating, and decision-level late fallback. In EON, Hybrid Fusion weights inputs according to real-time sensor quality indicators and modality-specific confidence values.

### Modality Dropout
A regularization and robustness technique applied during model training where specific sensor inputs (e.g., video or audio) are randomly zeroed out or masked. This forces the fusion architecture to maintain accurate predictions when one or more sensors fail or become occluded in real-world environments.

### Conflicting Affective Cues
A condition where distinct communicative modalities exhibit contradictory valence or arousal values (e.g., a smiling face paired with a hostile, strained vocal tone, indicating polite sarcasm or passive resistance). EON treats cross-modal divergence as an explicit diagnostic signal rather than averaging it into an erroneous "neutral" classification.

## 6. Human Activity Recognition and Spatial Perception

### Human Activity Recognition (HAR)
The automated detection and classification of human physical motions, postures, gestures, and interaction sequences using optical cameras, depth sensors, or wearable inertial sensors.

### Spatial Skeleton / Pose Estimation
The computer vision process of extracting 2D or 3D anatomical joint coordinates (e.g., nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) from video frames. Canonical frameworks include MediaPipe Pose (33 3D landmarks) and COCO Pose (17 2D landmarks).

### Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN)
A deep learning architecture designed for skeleton-based action recognition. Human joint coordinates form the spatial graph vertices (connected by anatomical bones), and temporal edges link corresponding joints across successive frames, enabling joint convolution over space and time.

### Interaction Taxonomy
The hierarchical categorization of human activities into:
1. **Basic Activities:** Unimodal individual biomechanical actions (e.g., standing, walking, sitting, bending).
2. **Interaction Activities:** Communicative bodily signals directed at a robot or human partner (e.g., waving, pointing, nodding, beckoning).
3. **Human-Object Activities:** Interactions involving physical tools or environmental objects (e.g., typing, carrying a box, reading a book).
4. **Social Activities:** Multi-person interpersonal activities (e.g., handshake, conversation, queuing).

### Non-Inferential Interpretation
The architectural principle governing EON's perception layers, establishing that computer vision models output strictly descriptive physical motion labels (e.g., "rapid forward stride toward robot"), without inferring unobserved psychological intentions or safety clearance.

## 7. Uncertainty Quantification and Temporal Dynamics

### Aleatoric Uncertainty
Statistical uncertainty arising from inherent stochasticity, noise, or physical degradation in sensor observations (e.g., motion blur, lens flare, acoustic reverberation, microphone clipping, low illumination). Aleatoric uncertainty cannot be reduced by collecting more training data, but must be quantified per observation.

### Epistemic Uncertainty
Model uncertainty arising from lack of knowledge, novel scenarios, out-of-distribution human expressions, or unseen demographics. Epistemic uncertainty reflects the model's unfamiliarity with the input and can theoretically be reduced with additional representative training data.

### Confidence Calibration
The degree to which a machine learning model's predicted probability $P(\hat{Y} = y | X) = p$ matches the true empirical likelihood of correctness. Uncalibrated modern neural networks frequently produce overconfident softmax probabilities ($p \approx 0.99$) even when making incorrect predictions.

### Temperature Scaling
A post-processing calibration technique for classification models where logit vectors $z$ are divided by a single learned scalar parameter $T > 0$ before applying softmax: $\hat{p}_i = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_j e^{z_j / T}}$, correcting systemic overconfidence without altering model rank ordering.

### Dirichlet Calibration
An advanced multi-class probability calibration method that fits a Dirichlet distribution to the logit outputs, modeling class-dependent biases and correlation structures more flexibly than temperature scaling.

### Temporal Smoothing / Windowing
Techniques used to filter high-frequency sensor jitter and momentary misclassifications across consecutive frames. Methods include Exponential Moving Average (EMA), sliding window majority voting, Hidden Markov Models (HMM), and causal temporal convolutions.

## 8. Robotics Safety and Architectural Boundaries

### Non-Authoritative Actuation (`is_authoritative_stop=False`)
A fundamental safety invariant of OVIC EON stating that outputs generated by emotion recognition, social reasoning, or activity classification modules are non-authoritative recommendations. Under no circumstances may an affective observation trigger or override safety-critical emergency stops, collision avoidance maneuvers, or robot kinematic limits. Physical robot safety is strictly governed by independent, deterministic, hardware-certified safety controllers (ISO 13849 / ISO 10218 / ISO 15066).

### Deterministic Safety Controller
The real-time, certifiable execution environment on a robot responsible for trajectory generation, torque limits, obstacle collision avoidance, and E-Stop monitoring. EON operates strictly as an advisory intelligence overlay communicating via non-blocking asynchronous event envelopes.
